## 1. Non-Flight Certification Notice

> [!CAUTION]
> **OPERATIONAL SAFETY DISCLAIMER:**  
> All traverses, hazard thresholds, velocity profiles, and power estimations generated by MarsWalk 2.0 / Project Atlas are **modeled algorithmic estimates**. They are **never designated as operationally safe, flight-certified, or human-astronaut approved**. Real Martian surface operations require multi-redundant telemetry validation, high-fidelity terramechanics simulations, and formal NASA JPL Mission Operations Directorate review.

## 2. Spatial Coverage & Resolution Boundaries

1. **Spatial Bounding Box:**
   - Planetary route planning is confined to the surveyed **Jezero Crater 20 m/px CTX DTM mosaic** ($250 \times 250$ raster cells, spanning $5,000\,\text{m} \times 5,000\,\text{m}$). Coordinates outside this spatial envelope are outside the verified terrain grid.
   - High-resolution micro-hazard evaluation is confined to the **Hawksbill Gap 1 m/px HiRISE DTM window** ($600 \times 600$ raster cells, spanning $600\,\text{m} \times 600\,\text{m}$). Coordinates outside this window fall back to 20m regional CTX elevation.
2. **Sub-Pixel Micro-Hazards:**
   - Slopes are computed using the Horn 8-neighbor gradient kernel at 20.0m and 1.0m pixel scales.
   - Hazards smaller than 1 meter—such as isolated $30\text{--}50\,\text{cm}$ sharp basaltic boulders, centimeter-scale fracture crevasses, or loose wheel-trapping regolith pockets—are not resolved by satellite photogrammetry and require onboard rover hazard avoidance cameras (Hazcams) during tactical execution.

## 3. Subsystem Model Assumptions & Approximations

### 3.1 Power Architecture: Solar Analogue vs. Real MMRTG
- **Physical Reality:** NASA's Perseverance rover is powered by a **Multi-Mission Radioisotope Thermoelectric Generator (MMRTG)** containing plutonium-238 dioxide, which continuously generates $\approx 110\,\text{W}$ of electrical power independent of sunlight or dust storms.
- **MarsWalk Model:** MarsWalk implements a **$900.0\,\text{W}$ rated clean solar array model** with plane-parallel Beer-Lambert optical extinction:
  $$T(\tau) = \exp\left(-\frac{\tau}{0.85}\right)$$
- **Purpose:** This model serves as an **analogue simulation platform** for solar-powered Martian surface exploration assets (e.g. MER Opportunity, InSight, and future solar-powered crew EVA rovers). It does not represent Perseverance's physical nuclear battery.

### 3.2 RF Dust Extenuation: Heuristic Proxy vs. Rayleigh Absorption
- **Physical Reality:** At microwave X-band frequencies ($8.4\,\text{GHz}$, $\lambda \approx 3.57\,\text{cm}$), Martian dust particles ($a \sim 1.5\,\mu\text{m}$) have a size parameter $x \approx 2.6 \times 10^{-4} \ll 1$. This places electromagnetic propagation deep in the **Rayleigh scattering regime**, where scattering is negligible ($< 10^{-14}\,\text{m}^2$) and absorption dominates. True atmospheric RF extinction at UHF and X-band is typically $< 0.05\,\text{dB/km}$ even during severe dust storms.
- **MarsWalk Model:** MarsWalk models UHF link attenuation at $\alpha_{\text{UHF}} \approx 0.008\,\text{dB/km}$ (transparent) and provides an illustrative empirical proxy formula for dust storm link degradation:
  $$\alpha_{\text{RF}} = 0.008 + 0.098 \cdot \tau^{1.15}\,\text{dB/km}$$
- **Purpose:** This formula serves as an **illustrative engineering proxy** to demonstrate environmental coupling to radio communications, and must not be cited as a first-principles derivation from Mie theory.

### 3.3 Mobility Energy Proxy
- **Model Equation:**
  $$E_{\text{mobility}} = \left(\frac{d}{3.5}\right) + (N_{\text{targets}} \times 11.25)\,\text{Wh}$$
- **Approximations:**
  - Assumes a constant specific energy consumption of $0.2857\,\text{Wh/m}$ ($\approx 286\,\text{Wh/km}$).
  - Real rover energy consumption varies dynamically based on wheel sinkage, slope grade, slip angle, turn-in-place maneuvers, and regolith shear strength (Bekker terramechanics).
  - Target dwell energy ($11.25\,\text{Wh}$) represents a nominal $\approx 45\text{-minute}$ instrument deployment at $\approx 15\,\text{W}$, which is an engineering estimate rather than a detailed instrument telemetry ledger.

## 4. Algorithmic & Simulation Boundaries

1. **Discrete Grid Routing:**
   - Multi-Objective A* searches over a discrete $20\text{m} \times 20\text{m}$ grid with 8-neighbor connectivity.
   - The rover's actual kinematic turning radius, crab-steering geometry, and continuous waypoint curvature are not computed during macroscopic path planning.
2. **Simplified Thermal Lumped Parameter:**
   - Battery survival heater power is approximated by an empirical linear relation:
     $$P_{\text{heater}} = \max\left(0.0, (-20.0 - T_{\text{air}}) \times 1.8\right)\,\text{W}$$
   - Internal thermal conduction, radiative heat loss, and waste heat recovery from onboard avionics are not explicitly modeled.
3. **Simulated DTN Protocol:**
   - The Delay-Tolerant Networking (DTN) module simulates RFC 9171 Bundle Protocol custodian storage in non-volatile flash memory for 6 representative telemetry bundles. It is a protocol simulation demonstrator rather than a running ION (Interplanetary Overlay Network) daemon.

## 5. Requirements for Operational Flight Certification

To transition MarsWalk from a competition-grade mission operations demonstrator to an operational flight-ready tool, the following engineering steps would be required:
1. **Dynamic Terramechanics:** Integrate Bekker-Wong soil-wheel interaction equations parameterized by in-situ wheel trenching measurements.
2. **Stereo Hazcam Point Cloud Ingestion:** Ingest 3D point clouds from rover navigation cameras (Navcams) and hazard cameras (Hazcams) for sub-meter obstacle avoidance.
3. **Continuous Spline Trajectory Generation:** Smooth discrete A* grid paths into clothoid or quintic polynomial splines respecting rover steering kinematics.
4. **SPICE Kernel Integration:** Couple orbital relay Line-of-Sight calculations directly to NAIF SPICE ephemeris kernels (`.bsp` and `.tls` files) for MRO, TGO, and Odyssey orbital passes.
5. **Radiation & Thermal Vacuum Verification:** Validate electronic subsystem models against environmental chamber testing data.
